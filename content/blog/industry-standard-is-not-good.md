If you think bad software gets a pass just because it is the "industry standard", then sorry to break it to you, but that is straight-up wrong.

Adobe is the best example here. I don't even have to say how bad they are at what they do.

Their apps suck, and their subscription policies suck even more.

## The problem with Adobe

Making software like Photoshop, Premiere Pro, and After Effects the "industry standard" is just straight-up BS when that label becomes the only reason people keep using it.

You know there are issues with the software. You know it sucks. You know the workflow is ass. But you still use it. Why?

Because it's the "industry standard."

Why should I, as a video editor who has spent my entire editing career using DaVinci Resolve because it was free, better, faster, and even cheaper if I wanted to buy it, have to switch to a new app just because people think Adobe is the industry standard?

I can do the same things I would do in Premiere, and sometimes even better and faster, in DaVinci Resolve.

I don't have to export from Premiere and then import into After Effects to colour grade, add motion graphics, etc. I can do all of that in DaVinci itself without switching to a different piece of software.

Yeah, Adobe has Dynamic Link, so you don't always have to export from Premiere and manually import into After Effects. But you still have to switch between apps.

## The standard becomes the lock

I'm not saying that anything that becomes the "industry standard" is bad.

Sometimes standards are great when the application is made in a good way & care about its users, but they can also lock people out of the industry if they never learned that specific software from the start.

If there is a replacement for a certain app that does the work i want & its even better & faster why cant i switch to that?

Why should someone be forced to use a shitty app just because some people decided that they want the "industry standard"?

I could not do a freelance work because the client wanted me to use Premier pro, Why does he want me to use that?? If he is paying me for the final edit why does he want me to specifically use Premier pro? that does not make sense.

This type of "industry standard" can kill creativity. It stops people from trying different apps and doing different things. It forces people to keep using the same thing even when they know it's bad.

It can also kill innovation if the market stays focused on that one app.

## Better alternatives already exist

What [Chike](https://x.com/zellzoi_design/status/2101701620329247228) did is exactly what we need more of.

Chike, made an alternative to After Effects, the "industry standard", and it works sooo good, & showed that it can work better.

We don't need an "industry standard". We just need apps that can get our work done, and do it better.

Affinity gives people a free alternative for photo editing, vector design, and page layout. Canva also made Cavalry free for 2D animation and motion graphics. Maxon made Autograph free as an alternative for motion graphics, compositing, VFX, and 2D/3D work.

And that still does not stop people from staying stuck with Adobe.

Want to know why?

Because Adobe is the "industry standard."

The same thing applies to web apps and services like Slack, Linear, and Discord.

If something becomes popular enough, it becomes the standard, and people tend to overlook the bad parts of it.

If we don't change the standard ourselves, no one else will do it.

Things like age verification on Discord and the unstable apps from Adobe are good examples. These companies can get away with things people dislike because they have become the standard.

"Industry standard" should describe what most people use.

It should never be an excuse to stop questioning whether the software is actually good.
